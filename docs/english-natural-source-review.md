# Natural conversation source review

Checked 7 October 2026. The bank now contains 26 EdAcc excerpts: the original
two plus 24 new ones. They are pilot content with explicit independent-listening
and difficulty-review warnings. Thirty clips are selectable across the regional
pools; six additional legacy/mixed clips are excluded from regional selection.

The current [EdAcc release](https://datashare.ed.ac.uk/handle/10283/8983)
provides conversations between friends, linguistic-background information,
transcripts and timed segments. The release lists CC BY-SA and includes the
[CC BY-SA 4.0 licence text](https://datashare.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/bc7c247e-ae85-41cc-91a8-a1af06f4da29/content).
Its archive is approximately 5.51 GB. The earlier release linked from the project
home page is withdrawn and superseded; use the current release.

The [README](https://datashare.ed.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/23371402-f1d6-46f3-b5d8-9104f207f83a/content)
documents linguistic_background.csv, transcripts and segments. It specifically
warns that example speaker-category labels are not reliable without verification.
Use speaker background evidence and listening review rather than assigning US/UK
labels from a file name or an automatic category.

Candidate intake procedure:

1. Record source, licence and attribution in english-recording-intake.csv.
2. Identify speakers and verify regional background; reject read-aloud segments.
3. Review for general workplace relevance across all roles. Conversation between
   friends alone does not establish suitability for recruitment assessment.
4. Select a coherent 120–210 second excerpt with complete sentence boundaries,
   intelligible sound and no dependence on specialist prior knowledge.
5. Write six original questions with transcript evidence, assess ambiguity and
   distinguish detail, purpose/attitude and inference demands.
6. Review transcript coherence and question evidence before adding a versioned
   pilot bank row. Record pending independent listening review in the inventory;
   complete that review before treating the content as production-ready.
   Retain source licence and modification credit with the stored audio/player.
7. Run the media audit and then include the recording in the all-role pilot.

AMI continues to provide useful workplace role-play content, but its mixed
speaker backgrounds require individual regional review. Existing VOA material
remains a clearly labelled US starter pool until reviewed natural replacements
are available.

## Current intake

The current official archive was downloaded and verified against its publisher's
MD5, `146b4b8026b5d0ce9611667c708456b3`. Excerpts are made from the original WAVs.
Speaker evidence comes from original `linguistic_background.csv`; timed evidence
comes from the original test/dev transcripts and segments. The publisher's
[dataset mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/edinburghcstr/edacc) helped locate
content, but its normalized first-language categories were not treated as a
substitute for the original self-reported fields.

- C08: three Scottish excerpts (childhood programmes, food/city life, holidays).
- C32_P2: two Southern London excerpts (learning/work, food/photos/future plans).
- C46_P1/P2: eight Scottish excerpts (coastal visits, remote travel, tourism,
  shared stays, cycling spaces, recycling, electric cars, family books).
- C64: eight American-accent/international excerpts (games, imaginative play,
  sport, books, television, reading, meals and snacks).
- C19: five self-described American-accent/international excerpts (meals,
  study/careers, independence/transport, renting and moving).

C64-A self-reports an American accent, English/Japanese first languages and
residence in Hawaii; C64-B self-reports Indonesian speech and Indonesian/Hokkien
first languages. C19-B self-reports an American accent and English/Shona first
languages, with residence in South Africa; C19-A reports Mandarin and a Chinese
or mixed accent. These are mixed-accent English conversations, not claims that
both speakers are US nationals or exclusively American-accent speakers.

C32 speakers report Southern London accents and English/Fante first languages;
C46 speakers report Glaswegian accents and English first language. C08 speakers
report Scottish/Fife and Scottish-inflected English accents. Questions depend
only on the spoken discussion; no prior film, book, travel or engineering
knowledge is required. Historical prices, pandemic references and personal
opinions are treated as listening content, not present-day factual advice.

All 26 question sets have transcript-backed editorial review and successful media
checks, but no independent human auditory sign-off or measured difficulty data.
C06 remains excluded as mixed British/transatlantic speech, and C31 remains
excluded because selected passages did not meet the intended clarity/topic scope.
The two existing scripted VOA recordings remain in the US pool and are labelled.
Do not count multiple clips as independent speaker diversity or claim the earlier
20–30-per-region target has been reached.
